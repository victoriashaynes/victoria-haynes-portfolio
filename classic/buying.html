Buying and Product Development Projects

As I have been working to receive my Bachelors Degree in Apparel Merchandising I have gained experience in retail buying projects involving assortment planning, sales analysis, and trend forecasting. I have become skilled in evaluating consumer preferences and market data to develop strategic product selections that align with brand identity and drive profitability.

Six Month Buy Plan Final Project

I developed a strategic six-month Spring/Summer 2026 buying plan for Ulta Beauty’s private-label lip category, leveraging sales history, trend forecasting, and market growth insights to guide assortment, pricing, and inventory decisions. My recommendation focused on a cohesive mix of lipsticks, glosses, and lip oils in Ulta’s top-performing seasonal shades—red, baby pink, and hot pink—balancing core classics with trend-driven items that appeal to a wide demographic. I planned for a 7% seasonal sales increase, aligned receipts with peak months, minimized markdown risk, and used industry-supported size and color distributions to optimize stock levels. With an average retail of $11 and a 70% markup strategy, this assortment reinforces Ulta’s commitment to quality and accessibility while supporting both everyday shoppers and younger “clean girl” beauty consumers. Overall, this plan reflects my ability to merge analytical forecasting with brand-aware merchandising to build an assortment that is profitable, data-backed, and trend-relevant for the Chicago market.

Buying Simulation Project

For this final project, I worked with a team in a simulated Assistant Buyer role for Ulta Beauty. We created a six-month buying plan for the Spring/Summer 2025 season, selecting a specific city, analyzing its market needs, and building an assortment aligned with trends and sales goals.

Our plan included sales forecasts, inventory levels, and markup/markdown strategies, all supported by data-driven reasoning. We then presented our recommendations to our Merchandise Manager (our professor), mirroring the real-world process of buyers pitching plans to their DMM.

This project strengthened my skills in forecasting, assortment planning, collaboration, and understanding the full buying process.

Textile Science Product Development Final Project

For this textile science product development project, I partnered with the Baylor Biking Club to identify the most durable and high-performing fabric for cycling shorts. Three textile blends were tested for abrasion resistance, weathering, UV exposure, pilling, and water behavior. The winning fabric, 83% recycled polyester and 17% spandex, stood out for its exceptional abrasion resistance, which is essential for frequent cycling. Although it showed lower water repellency, it dried significantly faster than the other options, making it more practical for active use. The fabric also demonstrated minimal color change under UV/weathering conditions and showed almost no pilling, ensuring longevity through repeated rides and washes. Its recycled composition further supports sustainable product development, making it both performance-ready and environmentally conscious.

Consumer Profile Project

For this consumer profile project, I developed a detailed understanding of a target customer by analyzing both hard goods and soft goods preferences, shopping behaviors, and lifestyle habits. I identified the products they are most likely to purchase, ranging from durable hard goods to everyday apparel and accessories, and explored how they prioritize quality, price, and brand values in their buying decisions. I also examined their shopping habits, including preferred retail channels—both in-store and online—and the types of stores they frequent. By combining insights into product preferences with behavioral patterns, this project provides a comprehensive picture of the consumer, helping inform merchandising, marketing strategies, and product development tailored to their needs.
